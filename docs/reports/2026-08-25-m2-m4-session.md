### CHE-117 (M4.2) — the singlet residual, attributed — `24f9a23`

`B3-PSF-SINGLET` carried an unmet gate (`2.21e-3` against a frozen `1.0e-3`) and
a negative control reading `0.42` against a `1.2` floor: the uniform,
pre-CHE-47 configuration agreeing with the analytic Airy oracle *better* than
the production weighted one. CHE-48 was opened to decompose that and closed
with no comment, no commit and no artifact. This decomposes it.

Two new probes, **O1 only**. O2 — our own float64 ASM/RS propagator — is not
consulted anywhere in this work; it is the oracle `L2-PSF-01` already had to
retire from this gate as circular validation, and nothing here readmits it.

**1. Not the sensor grid.** `benchmarks/probes/singlet_residual_grid.py` holds
the 128 µm physical window fixed and refines the sensor pitch through 1.0, 0.5,
0.25 and 0.125 µm — 6.49 to 51.88 pixels across the Airy radius, an 8×
refinement bracketing the frozen configuration on both sides. The weighted
residual reads `2.2072391812867093e-3` at every one of them, identical to ten
significant figures. This also retires half of CHE-103's contribution: the
2.44-px finding is about the separate pupil-to-focus grid, not this one.

**2. Not the centre correction.** `singlet_residual_attribution.py` substitutes
the hexapolar weight's two boundary multipliers one at a time. Removing the rim
half-weight reproduces the uniform arm to 0.099%; removing the central ray's
3/4 reproduces production to 0.26%. The two arms differ in exactly one place.

**3. And not the rim half-weight either — which is the finding.** Extending the
ladder to 3,148,801 rays shows the two arms converging to the same number:

| rings | rays | weighted vs O1 | uniform vs O1 | factor |
| --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 128 | 49,537 | 2.18905e-3 | 3.92895e-3 | 1.7948 |
| 256 | 197,377 | 2.20357e-3 | 1.14070e-3 | 0.5177 |
| 362 | 394,219 | 2.20601e-3 | 7.03878e-4 | 0.3191 |
| 512 | 787,969 | 2.20724e-3 | 9.21164e-4 | 0.4173 |
| 724 | 1,574,701 | 2.20785e-3 | 1.24973e-3 | 0.5660 |
| 1024 | 3,148,801 | 2.20816e-3 | 1.51637e-3 | 0.6867 |

The weighted arm is flat to 0.87% over 64× in ray count (and flat since 24
rings in the committed ladder). The uniform arm descends from `8.3e-2`, crosses
near 181 rings, bottoms out at `7.04e-4` at 362 rings, and climbs back. Its
`9.21e-4` — the number that clears the gate — is a point on a transient dip,
not a competing converged value.

So the control is **mis-specified, not backwards.** It compares two arms at one
ray count with neither required to be converged, and its verdict is a function
of that ray count: 10.7 at 8 rings, 1.79 at 128, 1.02 at 181, 0.42 at 512, 0.69
at 1024. A control whose sign flips with a numerical parameter is measuring
where two convergence curves cross rather than whether the weight helps.

Nothing was widened. The `1.0e-3` gate stays `NOT_MET` at `2.208e-3`, the `1.2`
floor is unchanged, and the control stays `KNOWN_FIRES_BACKWARDS` until it is
respecified to require convergence of both arms. The frozen tolerance bases in
`benchmarks/physics/L2-PSF-01/tolerances.yaml` were **appended to, not
reworded** — the verbatim-migration test caught the first attempt at rewriting
one — and the Python constants and the YAML were updated in lockstep so they
still match character for character.

out-claim the registry, the capability table or the ledger, that every path and

### CHE-115 (M3.3), partial — the gate's own metric, promoted — `2f4e9d7`

The substrate proof could not reproduce the frozen `2.2072e-3` for two declared
reasons, and one of them was wrong. Its docstring said the frozen gate is the
radial-profile residual in `psf_oracle_verification.py::_profile_residual`. It
is not: the committed number is produced by
`sensor_handoff_convergence.py::_relative_l2` — pixelwise, peak-normalised,
disc-masked. Promoting the function the docstring named would have centralised
the wrong computation.

Both are now named definitions in `src/verification/metrics.py`:
`disc_relative_l2_intensity` (with the `disc_mask` window it is evaluated over,
because the gate is the pair) and `radial_profile_relative_l2`.
`B3-PSF-SINGLET`'s `fft_oracle_intensity_relative_l2` metric now names the
first; it previously named `central_relative_l2_intensity`, a centred *square*
window with no peak normalisation, which is not the computation its own frozen
number came from. The promotion was proven behaviour-preserving rather than
assumed: the pre-move `_profile_residual` was extracted from git and run
against the promoted version on three sampling/centre configurations —
identical dicts, not approx-equal.

`MetricDefinition` gained `required_kwargs`, because a metric needing geometry
has no meaningful default for it and inventing one lets a caller get a number
for the wrong window without saying so; `support` is now validated against a
closed `SUPPORTS` vocabulary rather than free text. The register smoke test
asserts its geometry table covers exactly the metrics declaring
`required_kwargs`, so a new geometry-taking metric cannot be added and silently
skipped.

Also closes the `C_PLANAR_DOE_STEP` knowledge-pack gap in the claim ledger
(which the previous commit made due — `test_a_missing_knowledge_pack_is_filed_as_a_gap`
is designed to fail when a pack appears, and it did), and re-stamps
`singlet_residual_attribution.json`, whose probe received lint fixes after the
run that produced it.

## Scientific findings

1. **The `2.208e-3` singlet residual is converged in both refinable directions
   and is not caused by the quadrature weight.** The pre-CHE-47 uniform code was
   never more accurate; it was less converged in a direction that happened to
   cancel. The open question narrows from *why does the correct weight hurt* to
   *can an aberration-free paraxial oracle decide a real traced singlet at
   `1e-3` at all*. Separating the system's own aberration needs an oracle this
   family does not have, and O2 does not qualify.
2. **A control whose sign flips with a numerical parameter is measuring where
   two convergence curves cross.** Recorded against the control, not against
   the threshold.
3. **The disc window bounds the difference, not the normalisation.** Each
   pattern is divided by its *global* maximum, so a bright enough pixel outside
   the gate disc becomes the peak and rescales everything inside it — a stray
   5× corner pixel takes an exact agreement to `0.8`. The obvious reading of a
   windowed metric is that nothing outside matters, and that reading is wrong
   here: it is the difference between "the gate is blind to a halo" and "a halo
   silently corrupts the gate". Found while testing the promotion, now declared
   in the metric's `blind_to` and asserted.
4. **The frozen gate was not the metric the substrate proof said it was.**

What is **not** established: that `2.208e-3` is correct.
